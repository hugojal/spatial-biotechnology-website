## 1. Vision and Philosophy

Cells continuously sense their environment by integrating multiple input signals from neighbouring cells and respond to them with a fast-flowing cascade of abundant outputs. In turn, these outputs modify the microenvironment.

Taking a set of distinct cell types, this spatiotemporal combinatorial phenomenon can presumedly lead to a vast number of multicellular spatial patterns. Still, only a relatively limited number of stable multicellular spatial patterns with functions (and dysfunctions) emerge from all possible coordination events amongst multiple cell types.

For these patterns to repeatedly arise in distinct organisms, a set of principles driving the spatial organization of multiple cell types must be conserved. However, these principles remain largely unknown as the scientific community has only recently begun to deconstruct multicellular organization.

Being able to **read**, **anticipate**, and eventually **control** the principles behind the spatial organization of multiple cell types are some of the most fascinating problems of modern biotechnology. Advances in this area are key to manipulating tissue function and will bring transformative benefits to society:
- Deeper systems-level understanding of multicellular biology
- Next-generation cell-based adoptive immunotherapies
- High-precision multiplexed diagnostic tools
- Engineered tissues for precision and regenerative medicine

## 2. Mission in Cancer and Solid Tumors

In the **Spatial Biotechnology** group at IBEC, we make headway on this problem via the in-depth study of **clonal behaviors in solid tumors**. 

Solid tumors are highly heterogeneous ecosystems. A major driver fueling this heterogeneity is **clonal diversity**, encompassing genetic mutations, copy number variations, and epigenetically regulated cell states. Clonal diversity critically dictates:
1. Tumor growth kinetics and architecture
2. Metastatic dissemination
3. Therapeutic resistance and immune evasion

Cell fate is intimately connected to local neighborhood geometry. However, rules governing clonal spatial organization remain obscure due to the lack of mature experimental and computational frameworks capable of tracking clones in their native 3D/spatial tissue context.

**Our Goal:**  
Develop high-throughput and spatial perturbation technologies to understand how cellular clones spatially organize to drive solid tumor development, designing better cancer therapies and predictive clinical tools.

## 3. Technology Pillars & Platforms

Our interdisciplinary group combines wet-lab synthetic biology with computational spatial data science:

1. **Multiplexed Ion Beam Imaging (MIBI) & Spatial Proteomics:**
   - Detection of 40+ isotopic mass-tagged antibodies simultaneously at sub-cellular resolution.
   - Histopathology-grade deep phenotyping of tumor microenvironments.
2. **Super-Resolution Multiplexed Microscopy:**
   - Sub-diffraction spatial interrogation of single-cell nuclear and subcellular architecture.
3. **High-Throughput Isotopic Bead Barcoding:**
   - Mass-spectrometric serological and secretome multiplexing.
4. **Pooled Genetic Perturbations & Genome Editing:**
   - In vivo clonal barcoding and pooled CRISPR screens in murine cancer models.
5. **3D In Vitro Tumor Organoids & Assay Automation:**
   - High-content robotic screens of clonal interactions in metabolically defined microenvironments.
6. **Computational Spatial Cartography:**
   - Graph neural networks, spatial point-process statistics, neighborhood cell-cell interaction modeling.
