# Research Lines

The Spatial Biotechnology Laboratory focuses on 4 primary research axes at the intersection of spatial multi-omics, tumor ecology, metabolic engineering, and synthetic immunology.

---

## Line 1: Clonal Cooperation & Tumor Evolution In Vivo

### Question:
**How is clonal cooperation established and maintained in solid tumors?**

### Background & Approach:
Widespread clonal heterogeneity in human tumors suggests that multifaceted ecological interactions govern tumor development beyond simple Darwinian competition. Emerging evidence reveals that tumor evolution is frequently driven by **clonal cooperation**, where mutualistic growth equilibrium between distinct subclones provides optimal population fitness.

### Methodology:
- High-complexity pooled genomic barcoding and CRISPR perturbations.
- In vivo murine solid tumor models tracking tens to hundreds of genetically unique clones.
- Subcellular MIBI and super-resolution multiplexed imaging of clonal interfaces in native tissue architecture.

### Impact:
Characterizing molecular and physical determinants that promote clonal cooperation, deciphering causal mechanisms of subclonal spatial patterning and therapy resilience.

---

## Line 2: Metabolic Microenvironment & Subclonal Growth

### Question:
**How does metabolite accessibility regulate the growth of neighbouring cancer cell clones?**

### Background & Approach:
Solid tumors exhibit extreme local biochemical gradients—hypoxia, nutrient starvation, localized lactate accumulation, and differential vascularization. Metabolic reprogramming of cancer cells creates microenvironmental niches that exert intense selective pressure, shifting subclonal clonal representation.

### Methodology:
- 3D in vitro tumor organoid and spheroid microtissues.
- High-throughput automated liquid handling and robotic screening platforms.
- Metabolically defined microfluidic cultures combined with multiplex spatial imaging and spatial metabolomics/proteomics.

### Impact:
Mapping subclonal metabolic dependencies to identify vulnerabilities where targeting microenvironmental metabolite access can disrupt aggressive clonal expansion.

## Line 3: Clonal Populations & Microenvironments in Primary Patient Tumors

### Question:
**How do clonal populations and microenvironments evolve in human primary tumors?**

### Background & Approach:
The tumor microenvironment (TME)—composed of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), vascular networks, myeloid cells, lymphocytes, and extracellular matrix (ECM)—co-evolves with malignant subclones.

### Methodology:
- Single-cell spatial pharmacobiology and spatial transcriptomics / histopathology-grade MIBI.
- Deep spatial profiling of human patient cohorts in collaboration with clinical centers (IDIBAPS, Stanford, DKFZ).
- Mapping physical barriers (e.g., periostin-rich ECM networks, FAP+ fibroblast barriers) impeding drug penetration and immune surveillance.

### Impact:
Discovering spatial biomarkers correlating with patient prognosis and identifying mechanical/stromal barriers to maximize therapeutic antibody delivery.

---

## Line 4: Next-Generation Adoptive T-Cell Transfer Therapies

### Question:
**How can adoptive T-cell transfer therapies be engineered to overcome solid tumor immunosuppression?**

### Background & Approach:
Adoptive T-cell therapies (CAR-T, TCR-T, TILs) demonstrate remarkable efficacy in hematologic malignancies but face severe obstacles in solid tumors: restricted infiltration, dense fibrotic stroma, and rapid functional exhaustion.

### Methodology:
- Synthetic immune cell engineering and modular receptor redesign.
- In situ spatial tracking of engineered T cells at single-cell resolution in tissue-permissive tumor models.
- Multiplexed interrogation of T-cell functional persistence, exhaustion states, and spatial interaction with tumor subclusters.

### Impact:
Uncovering molecular determinants of productive T-cell fitness and persistence to engineer resilient cell-based therapeutics for solid cancer patients.
